# DoCast — Do-Operator Forecasting

## Intervention-Valid Scenario Forecasting for MISO Time Series with Controllable Covariates

> **Version**: v1.0 (2026-06-12). First draft, pre-G0 (the prior-art sweep in §7.1 is **binding** before any code is written). This document is the single source of truth for the project.
>
> **Thesis in one sentence**: Modern MISO forecasters (TFT, TimeXer, NBEATSx, TiDE) consume known-future *controllable* covariates — prices, promotions — as ordinary conditioning features; but the only reason those covariates exist in the input contract is to answer **what-if scenario queries**, which are *interventional* (`do(a)`), not observational (`| a`). Because the covariate paths in the training data are generated by a *policy that reacts to demand drivers*, observational training produces forecasts that can be **accurate as predictions yet systematically wrong as scenarios** — and the field has never measured this, let alone fixed it.
>
> **Project goal**: (i) the first **Scenario Validity Audit** of SOTA MISO forecasters on public benchmarks; (ii) an identification theory mapping direct multi-horizon forecasting onto g-computation; (iii) **DoCast**, an architecture-agnostic training framework (structural response head + sequential orthogonalization) that restores intervention validity at negligible observational-accuracy cost; (iv) a released evaluation protocol (semi-synthetic confounding dial + natural-experiment holdouts + policy-ranking metrics). Target: ICLR 2027 (method + theory + audit) or KDD 2027 research track (applied framing).
>
> **Hard disjointness constraint (binding)**: this project must not overlap PRISM (`experiments/PRISM/`) on any of its axes — no regime tracking, no heterogeneous expert routing, no MoE, no CI/CD gating, no routing-level or any other drift adaptation, no test-time adaptation, no spectral regime descriptors, no online model selection. DoCast is a *single-model, train-once, architecture-agnostic* method about the **causal semantics of covariates**, not about *which architecture to use when*. See §6.4 for the axis-by-axis disjointness table.

## 0. TL;DR

- **Problem**: asymmetric MISO scenario forecasting — given history and a *candidate* future path of controllable covariates `a′` (a price ladder, a promotion plan), forecast the target under `do(a′)` (§1).
- **Gap**: the deep-forecasting literature fuses known-future covariates ever more cleverly (TFT gating, TimeXer cross-attention, NBEATSx basis expansion) but always estimates the *observational* conditional. The longitudinal causal-inference literature (MSMs, CRN, G-Net, Causal Transformer) estimates interventional quantities but with binary/low-dimensional treatments, short horizons, hundreds of units — and is never evaluated as a forecaster. Neither side measures the **accuracy–validity frontier** (§2, §6).
- **Core hypothesis (falsifiable)**: under realistic, empirically calibrated confounding (promotions planned on anticipated demand), SOTA MISO forecasters exhibit material scenario bias — wrong elasticity magnitudes and frequently wrong *signs* — that grows with confounding strength, while their observational test error is unaffected (the benchmark is structurally blind to the failure) (§3, H1).
- **Gate experiment (cheap, run first)**: the **Scenario Validity Audit** (§7.2) — a semi-synthetic M5-calibrated generator with a confounding dial γ and known ground-truth elasticities; train DLinear-MISO / TimeXer / TFT; measure elasticity bias vs γ. If SOTA models are already approximately intervention-valid at realistic γ̂, the project pivots or dies at ~2 weeks' sunk cost (§8).
- **Method (DoCast, §5)**: covariate typing (controllable `a` vs exogenous-known `c` vs past-only `x`) → a **functional-coefficient structural head** `ŷ = m(H,c) + Θ(H,c)·φ(a)` emitted by any backbone → **sequentially cross-fitted orthogonalization** of the treatment path (Neyman-orthogonal R-learner loss, purged temporal folds) → optional balancing regularizer (ablation only). No mixture, no router, no adaptation loop.
- **Theory (§4)**: Proposition 1 — a direct multi-horizon forecaster is a plug-in g-computation estimator iff the covariate policy is *plan-ahead* and *ignorable given the model's input set*; Proposition 2 — scenario bias decomposes into hidden-confounding and within-horizon-feedback terms with separate diagnostics; Proposition 3 — orthogonality of the proposed loss (first-order insensitivity to policy-model error).
- **Battlefields (§7.3)**: M5 (store-item prices, SNAP, events) and Favorita (promotions, holidays, oil, the 2016-04-16 earthquake) — both already in `input/` — plus a released semi-synthetic suite. Natural-experiment holdouts (SNAP calendars, chain-wide reprices) provide *real-data* causal validation without counterfactual ground truth.
- **Rigor**: preregistered greenlight/kill criteria, naive anchors mandatory (including a zero-elasticity forecaster), multi-seed, DM + Wilcoxon + BH-FDR, purge/embargo splits, identification-strength diagnostics before any causal claim.

## 1. Problem Statement: Scenario-MISO Forecasting

### 1.1 Sample contract

At time `t`, with lookback `L` and horizon `H`, one decision unit (e.g., item × store) contributes:

| Tensor | Shape | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `x_target` | `[L]` | history | target history (sales, demand, load) |
| `x_past_cov` | `[L, C_p]` | history | past-only covariates (transactions, oil price, inventory proxies) |
| `c` | `[L+H, C_c]` | **exogenous known-future** | calendar, holidays, SNAP schedules — known in advance *and* not chosen in response to the target |
| `a` | `[L+H, C_a]` | **controllable known-future** | price path, promotion flags, markdown schedule — known in advance *because someone decides them* |
| `static` | `[C_s]` | static | item / store / category id |
| `y` | `[H]` | target | future target |

**Two distinct queries share this contract:**

- **Observational (what the literature trains and tests)**: `f_obs(H_t, c, a) ≈ E[y | H_t, c, a]` — "given that the policy chose `a`, what will sales be?"
- **Interventional (what the covariates are *for*)**: `Q(a′) = E[y | H_t, c, do(a_{t+1:t+H} = a′)]` — "if we *set* the price path to `a′`, what will sales be?"

These coincide only under identification conditions (§4) that real benchmark data demonstrably need not satisfy. **The covariate typing itself (a vs c vs x) is absent from every deep-forecasting benchmark and is our first, small, conceptual contribution**: M5's `sell_prices` and Favorita's `onpromotion` are `a`-type; SNAP and holidays are `c`-type; the literature treats all of them identically.

### 1.2 Why MISO-first

1. **Deployment realism**: scenario planning is per-decision-unit and single-target — a category manager prices *one* item at *one* store and asks for *its* demand curve. The natural output is one target series conditioned on many inputs.
2. **The asymmetry is causal, not just architectural**: in symmetric multivariate forecasting there is no "treatment"; the controllable-vs-exogenous distinction only exists once one channel is the target and others are decision variables. MISO is not a protocol choice here — it is the setting in which the scientific question is even well-posed.
3. **Headroom honesty**: we do not claim headline accuracy gains on observational test sets (those are saturated and, by H1, structurally blind to the failure we study). The headroom is on a *different axis* — scenario validity — where current SOTA has, to our knowledge, never been measured at all.

### 1.3 What this is NOT

Not treatment-effect estimation for its own sake (we require full forecast functionality and competitive observational accuracy); not counterfactual *explanation* of past outcomes (CausalImpact-style post-hoc analysis); not policy *optimization* (we stop at intervention-valid forecasts and a policy-ranking metric; closing the loop to pricing optimization is explicitly out of scope and listed as future work).

## 2. Motivation and Status-Quo Critique

| # | Implicit mainstream assumption | Evidence against it | DoCast's position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Known-future covariates are *ordinary inputs*; better fusion (gating, cross-attention) = better model | Prices and promotions are generated by a **policy that reacts to demand drivers** (anticipated seasonality, inventory, competitor moves). Conditioning on a policy's output ≠ intervening on it — a 60-year-old lesson (Haavelmo; Pearl) absent from the TSF benchmark culture | Type the covariates (§1.1); train so that the model's response to `a` estimates the *interventional* response |
| Q2 | Accuracy on observational test windows certifies usefulness | The deployment query is **off-policy**: "what if we discount 20% *when we normally wouldn't*?" Observational test sets never visit those (history, action) pairs, so test error cannot detect scenario bias — a benchmark blind spot, not a tail risk | Evaluate on interventional queries: semi-synthetic counterfactuals with ground truth + natural-experiment holdouts (§7.5) |
| Q3 | Benchmark covariate processes are policy-neutral givens | M5 promotions cluster around events; markdowns respond to realized sales (within-horizon feedback); Favorita promotions correlate with holidays and oil-shock periods. None of this has been audited | Measure the confounding structure of the benchmarks themselves (part of G1); publish the audit regardless of method outcome |
| Q4 | If causality matters, port a medical ITE-over-time method | CRN / G-Net / Causal Transformer assume binary or low-dim treatments, horizons of ~5–20 steps, O(10²–10³) units, and are **never benchmarked on forecast accuracy**; their balancing objectives are known to *sacrifice* predictive fit. Nobody has measured what validity costs in accuracy | Treat accuracy and validity as a **joint frontier**; require the method to sit on its Pareto face, not at either degenerate end |
| Q5 | Elasticity is a scalar to be estimated once (econometric tradition) | Demand response is heterogeneous in item, store, season, horizon distance, and history; log-log panel regressions average it away; deep forecasters could express it but are not trained to identify it | A functional-coefficient head `Θ(H,c)` = horizon-indexed, history-conditional response surface — interpretable *and* identified (§5) |

**Root cause shared by Q1–Q5**: the field optimizes `E[y | a]` while selling `E[y | do(a)]`. DoCast's entire contribution is to state the difference, measure it, and close it.

## 3. Hypotheses (all falsifiable, all preregistered)

| ID | Statement | Test | Falsified if |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1 (audit)** | SOTA MISO forecasters trained observationally exhibit material scenario bias under realistic confounding: elasticity estimation error and sign-error rate grow monotonically with confounding strength γ, while observational test error stays flat | G1 semi-synthetic dial study (§7.2) | At empirically calibrated γ̂, scenario bias of all SOTA baselines is within the seed-noise band (models are "accidentally causal") |
| **H2 (frontier)** | There is a nontrivial accuracy–validity trade-off: observational SOTA sits at one extreme (accurate, invalid), ported ITE-over-time methods at the other (valid-ish, inaccurate); the frontier between them is measurable and currently unoccupied | Joint (WRMSSE, elasticity-RMSE) scatter for all baselines (§7.5) | CRN/G-Net/Causal-Transformer ports match SOTA observational accuracy out of the box (no frontier — just adopt them) |
| **H3 (method)** | A structural response head + sequential orthogonalization Pareto-dominates both extremes: ≥ 50% reduction in scenario-bias metrics vs the observational backbone at ≤ 2% relative observational-accuracy loss | D2 vs D0/D1 and vs causal imports (§5.3, §7.5) | D2 fails the 50%/2% condition on the calibrated-γ̂ semi-synthetic; or orthogonalization adds nothing over the head alone (D2 ≈ D1) |
| **H4 (decomposition)** | Scenario bias separates into (i) hidden-confounding bias and (ii) within-horizon-feedback bias, with distinct diagnostics and distinct fixes; benchmarks differ in their mixture (M5 markdowns ≈ feedback-heavy; Favorita promotions ≈ confounding-heavy) | Generator with independent dials (γ, δ); diagnostic estimators of Prop. 2 applied to real data | The two dials produce statistically indistinguishable bias signatures (decomposition has no empirical content) |
| **H5 (real-data validity)** | On natural-experiment holdouts (SNAP calendar shifts, chain-wide reprices, earthquake window), DoCast's implied effects are closer to quasi-experimental (DiD) estimates than the observational backbone's | §7.5 natural-experiment protocol | DoCast's natural-experiment error is not significantly smaller (DM + BH-FDR) than D0's on any battlefield |

## 4. Theoretical Framing

### 4.1 Direct multi-horizon forecasting as plug-in g-computation

Let `V_t = (H_t, c)` be the model's full conditioning set at forecast origin `t` and `Y(a′)` the potential outcome path under `do(a_{t+1:t+H} = a′)`.

> **Proposition 1 (identification; to be formalized).** If
> **(A1, plan-ahead policy)** the realized path `a` is determined at origin: `a = π(V_t, U_π)` with policy noise `U_π`, i.e., no component of `a` responds to outcomes realized inside the horizon; and
> **(A2, ignorability given the input set)** `U_π ⫫ Y(a′) | V_t` — every common cause of the action path and the outcome path is measurable w.r.t. what the model actually ingests;
> then `E[y | V_t, a = a′] = E[Y(a′) | V_t]`: the direct forecaster *is* a valid scenario engine, and conversely any violation of A1/A2 makes the observational conditional unidentified as a scenario.

The value of stating this triviality precisely is the **failure-mode taxonomy** it induces:

- **(V1) Hidden confounding** — the planner sees signals not in `V_t` (internal demand forecasts, inventory positions, competitor intel). Bias term ∝ the partial covariance between policy residual and outcome residual given `V_t`.
- **(V2) Within-horizon feedback** — markdowns react to realized sales at `t+k`. Then conditioning on the *whole* path `a` conditions on descendants of intermediate outcomes; the valid object is the sequential g-formula, and **no amount of feature engineering at origin can fix a direct forecaster** — the estimand itself must change (origin-decided paths only) or the factorization must become sequential. To our knowledge this distinction (plan-ahead vs adaptive components of "known-future" covariates) has never been articulated in the deep-forecasting literature, where "known future" is treated as a single category.

> **Proposition 2 (bias decomposition; to be formalized).** Under a partially linear outcome model, the scenario bias of the observational conditional decomposes additively into a V1 term (estimable from the policy-residual/outcome-residual covariance) and a V2 term (estimable from the dependence of future actions on intermediate outcome innovations), each with a plug-in diagnostic computable on real benchmark data.

Prop. 2's diagnostics are deliverables in their own right: they let us *score benchmarks* by confounding structure (H4) — a reusable community artifact.

### 4.2 Orthogonal multi-horizon learning

We adapt double/debiased machine learning to the path-treatment, multi-horizon setting. With treatment basis `φ(a) ∈ R^B` (e.g., per-horizon log price ratio to a reference, promo indicators), outcome nuisance `m(V_t) ≈ E[ψ(y) | V_t]`, and policy nuisance `π(V_t) ≈ E[φ(a) | V_t]`, the **R-learner-style multi-horizon loss** is

```
L_ortho(θ) = Σ_h [ (ψ(y)_h − m̂_h(V_t)) − Θ_θ,h(V_t)ᵀ (φ(a) − π̂(V_t))_h ]²
```

with nuisances `m̂, π̂` fitted by **temporally purged K-fold cross-fitting** (folds are contiguous time blocks; an embargo ≥ H separates nuisance-fit data from loss-evaluation data — the same purge/embargo hygiene the repo already uses for chronological splits).

> **Proposition 3 (orthogonality; to be formalized).** `L_ortho` is Neyman-orthogonal in `(m, π)`: first-order insensitive to nuisance estimation error, giving the response head a fast rate even when nuisances converge slowly (lineage: Chernozhukov et al. 2018; Nie & Wager 2021 R-learner; Foster & Syrgkanis 2023 orthogonal statistical learning; Lewis & Syrgkanis dynamic-DML for the sequential/V2 extension).

**Honest structural caveat (stated up front, ablated in §7.6)**: linearity in `φ(a)` is a model assumption. Mitigations: per-horizon coefficients, basis expansion (piecewise-linear price grids, promo × depth interactions), and a misspecification ablation against a nonlinear-truth generator. The trade — a restricted response class in exchange for identification and orthogonality — *is the paper's thesis applied to itself* and will be presented as such.

### 4.3 Classical lineage

Haavelmo (1943) on policy interventions vs conditioning; Robins' g-formula and marginal structural models (the V2 analysis is a g-computation argument); Pearl's do-calculus (backdoor conditions = A2); econometric demand estimation (log-log elasticity panels, BLP as remote ancestry); modern DML/orthogonal learning (§4.2). DoCast is the transplant of this lineage into the direct multi-horizon deep-forecasting contract — the novelty is the *mapping* and the *measurement*, and we will say so plainly rather than dress the g-formula up as new.

## 5. Method Sketch: DoCast

### 5.1 Dataflow overview

```
x_target[L], x_past_cov[L,C_p], c[L+H,C_c]          a[L+H,C_a]  (controllable path)
        │                                                │
        ▼                                                ▼
   Backbone encoder  (ANY: TimeXer / TFT / TiDE /   Treatment basis φ(a)[H,B]
   DLinear-MISO — frozen choice per experiment;     (log price ratio, promo flags,
   DoCast adds NO new backbone)                      depth × duration interactions)
        │                                                │
        ├──► m_θ(V_t) ∈ R^H        baseline forecast     │
        │    (response under reference action ā)         │
        └──► Θ_θ(V_t) ∈ R^{H×B}    response surface      │
                     │                                   │
                     ▼                                   ▼
        ŷ(a′) = m_θ(V_t) + Θ_θ(V_t) · φ(a′)     ← scenario query API: any a′
                     │
   Training: L_ortho with cross-fitted nuisances m̂ (outcome) and π̂ (policy),
             temporally purged folds, embargo ≥ H        (§4.2)
   Optional: balancing regularizer (CRN-style adversarial independence) —
             ablation arm only, expected to trade accuracy (§5.3 D3)
```

Single model, trained once, offline. No mixture-of-experts, no router, no regime state, no test-time adaptation, no online updating of any kind.

### 5.2 Components

1. **Covariate typing protocol (COVTYPE).** A documented, released annotation of every covariate in every battlefield dataset as `a` / `c` / `x`, with the plan-ahead-vs-adaptive sub-typing of `a` (Prop. 1). This is cheap, reviewable, and reusable; it also forces the benchmark-audit honesty of Q3.
2. **Structural response head (SHEAD).** The backbone's representation feeds two heads: `m_θ` (forecast under the reference action, e.g., regular price, no promotion) and `Θ_θ` (per-horizon response coefficients). Interpretability deliverable: `Θ` *is* a horizon-indexed, history-conditional elasticity surface — directly plottable, directly comparable to econometric estimates and to generator ground truth.
3. **Sequential orthogonalization (ORTHO).** Nuisances: `π̂` by LightGBM or a small MLP on `V_t` (the "policy model" — itself an interesting object: how predictable are the benchmark's pricing decisions?); `m̂` by the same backbone class trained on reference-action windows or by local-linear residualization. Purged temporal cross-fitting per §4.2.
4. **Balancing arm (BAL, ablation only).** Adversarial independence of the representation from `φ(a)` (CRN lineage). Included to *measure* the accuracy cost of balancing vs orthogonalization, not as a recommended component.
5. **Within-horizon feedback handling (V2 arm).** Where the `a`-typing flags adaptive components (markdowns): (i) restrict the scenario API to origin-decided paths (estimand honesty), and (ii) a sequential variant factorizing the horizon into blocks with re-conditioning (dynamic-DML style) — scoped as an extension; the core paper ships with (i) plus the V2 diagnostic.

### 5.3 Degeneration table (= built-in ablation matrix)

| Config | Head | Orthogonalization | Balancing | Equivalent to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D0 | — (backbone as-is) | ✗ | ✗ | observational SOTA (TimeXer/TFT/TiDE/DLinear-MISO) |
| D0-zero | — | ✗ | ✗ | **zero-elasticity anchor**: same backbone, `a` channels ablated — if D0 ≈ D0-zero on observational metrics, the covariates were decorative all along (degeneracy check) |
| D1 | SHEAD | ✗ | ✗ | varying-coefficient forecaster (structure without identification) |
| **D2 = DoCast** | SHEAD | ORTHO | ✗ | the method |
| D3 | SHEAD | ORTHO | BAL | + balancing (accuracy-cost probe) |
| C-CRN / C-GNet / C-CT | — | — | per their papers | ported ITE-over-time methods, evaluated as forecasters |
| E-FE | — | — | — | econometric anchor: log-log fixed-effects elasticity panel + seasonal-naive base forecast |
| E-DML | — | ✓ (GBM nuisances) | — | two-stage tabular DML (no deep backbone): the "is deep learning even needed" anchor |

Hypothesis mapping: H1 → D0 vs generator truth; H2 → D0 vs C-* on the joint frontier; H3 → D2 vs D0/D1/C-*; H4 → dial study + diagnostics; H5 → D2 vs D0 on natural experiments. The kill criterion for ORTHO is D2 ≈ D1 (preregistered).

### 5.4 Backbone-agnosticism

DoCast is a *head + loss*, instantiated on ≥ 3 backbones (TimeXer, TFT, DLinear-MISO) to demonstrate the claim is about training semantics, not architecture. This is the structural opposite of an architecture paper — and deliberately so, since the repo's other project (PRISM) owns the architecture-selection axis.

The bottom-right joint cell is the gap. Adjacent-but-distinct work we will engage head-on rather than cite away:

### 6.3 Contribution statement (three sentences)

1. **An unmeasured failure mode**: the first audit showing that SOTA MISO forecasters' scenario responses are systematically confounded on realistic data — and that observational benchmarks are structurally incapable of detecting it (H1, Figure-1 material regardless of method outcome).
2. **A principled bridge**: identification conditions (plan-ahead + input-set ignorability) under which direct multi-horizon forecasting *is* g-computation, a bias decomposition with computable diagnostics, and an orthogonal training objective that any existing backbone can adopt.
3. **A reusable yardstick**: the accuracy–validity frontier plus a released protocol (covariate typing, confounding-dial semi-synthetics, natural-experiment holdouts, policy-ranking metrics) that lets the community evaluate the *decision usefulness* of covariate-aware forecasters rather than their observational fit alone.

## 7. Experimental Design

### 7.1 G0 — Prior-art sweep (binding; ~3 days; before any code)

Systematic search (arXiv, OpenReview, DBLP; 2023–2026) for: "causal/counterfactual demand forecasting", "intervention(al) forecasting", "price elasticity neural/deep", "scenario forecasting validity", "what-if time series", "orthogonal learning forecasting", plus citation-chasing from CRN/Causal-Transformer/dynamic-DML and from TimeXer/TFT. **Kill/adjust rule**: if a 2024–2026 paper already (i) audits SOTA forecasters interventionally on public benchmarks *or* (ii) proposes an orthogonal training objective for multi-horizon path treatments with a forecast-accuracy requirement, the overlapping contribution is dropped and the proposal re-scoped (the audit alone, the theory alone, or a head-to-head extension). Outcome logged in this document as v1.1 before G1 starts.

### 7.2 G1 — Gate experiment: the Scenario Validity Audit (~1.5 weeks)

- **Generator (released)**: an M5-calibrated structural simulator per unit: latent demand `d_t` (trend + weekly/annual seasonality + event uplifts + AR noise); `log E[sales_t] = β₀ + d_t + ε·snap_t + Θ*·log(p_t/p̄) (+ nonlinear variant)`; sales ~ negative binomial; **confounding dial γ**: pricing policy sets promotions/markdowns with probability increasing in *anticipated* `d_{t+1:t+H}` (V1); **feedback dial δ**: markdown probability increasing in realized excess of past sales vs plan (V2). Ground-truth `Θ*` known per unit; marginals calibrated to real M5 statistics (promo frequency, price-change frequency, demand CV).
- **Calibration of realistic γ̂**: estimated from real M5/Favorita via the Prop. 2 diagnostics (lead-lag correlation between promotion onset and demand innovations; policy-model predictability of `a` from `V_t`).
- **Models**: DLinear-MISO, TimeXer (vendored TSLib), TFT (`pytorch-forecasting`); anchors: seasonal-naive, D0-zero, E-FE, E-DML. Seeds {2021, 2022, 2023}. Device per repo policy (CUDA → MPS → CPU).
- **Measurements**: per-model elasticity estimate (finite-difference scenario probes ∂ŷ/∂log p at matched histories) vs `Θ*`: elasticity RMSE, **sign-error rate**, counterfactual WMAPE on held-out `do`-paths; observational WMAPE on standard test windows; both as functions of γ (and δ separately).
- **Preregistered greenlight rule**: greenlight iff, at γ̂, **(i)** every SOTA baseline shows sign-error ≥ 20% *or* elasticity bias ≥ 3× its seed-level std, **(ii)** observational test error is flat in γ (the blindness claim), and **(iii)** E-DML recovers ≥ 30% of the bias (the gap is *recoverable by orthogonalization*, not pure noise). Anchor hygiene (M1a lesson imported from the repo): if D0 ≈ D0-zero observationally, the dataset's covariates are decorative and that battlefield is disqualified for method claims.
- **Pivot rule**: (i) fails → "forecasters are accidentally causal" negative-result short paper (still informative: *why* — e.g., the conditioning set V_t happens to block the backdoors the generator implements); (iii) fails → the bias is real but not orthogonalization-recoverable → re-scope to audit + diagnostics paper (no method claims). Both pivots cost ≤ 2 weeks total.
- This audit is Figure 1 of the eventual paper regardless of outcome.

### 7.4 Baselines (all under identical protocol)

- **Observational SOTA (D0 forms)**: TimeXer, TFT, NBEATSx (`neuralforecast`), TiDE-with-covariates, DLinear-MISO, PatchTST-MISO (covariate-blind lower bound).
- **Causal imports**: CRN, G-Net, Causal Transformer (adapted to continuous path treatments where feasible; adaptation choices documented and stress-tested — we will be *more* careful with their ports than their papers were with forecasting, and say so).
- **Econometric/tabular**: E-FE (log-log fixed-effects elasticity + seasonal-naive base), E-DML (LightGBM nuisances, linear response).

### 7.5 Evaluation protocol

- **Splits**: strictly chronological, purge + embargo ≥ H at boundaries; normalization from train only; lookback tuned per method; seeds {2021, 2022, 2023}, mean ± std.
- **Observational metrics**: WRMSSE (M5 official weights), WMAPE, MASE, P50/P90 quantile loss.
- **Interventional metrics** (the new axis):
  - *Semi-synthetic*: counterfactual WMAPE on held-out `do`-paths; elasticity RMSE vs `Θ*`; sign-error rate (SER).
  - *Policy-ranking fidelity (PRF)*: Kendall-τ between model-implied and ground-truth ordering of K = 10 candidate price/promo plans per unit — the decision-relevant readout (a planner picks a plan; absolute calibration matters less than ranking).
  - *Natural experiments (real data)*: model-implied SNAP-day uplift vs a DiD estimate exploiting cross-state SNAP-calendar variation (M5); model-implied promo uplift vs holiday-orthogonalized promo episodes and chain-wide (plausibly cost-driven) reprices (Favorita); earthquake-window robustness (Favorita). Report **natural-experiment error (NEE)** = |model-implied effect − quasi-experimental estimate|.
- **Identification-strength diagnostics reported before any causal claim**: action-path variation per unit, policy-model predictability R² (overlap proxy), Prop. 2 V1/V2 diagnostics per dataset. Where identification is weak, claims are confined to the semi-synthetic suite — stated in the paper, not buried.
- **Statistics (house style)**: Diebold-Mariano for pairwise forecast comparisons, Wilcoxon signed-rank across units/windows, Benjamini-Hochberg FDR across the dataset × horizon × metric grid; no "win" claimed without surviving FDR.
- **Sensitivity analysis**: Rosenbaum-style hidden-confounding curves for the real-data effect estimates (how strong must an unobserved confounder be to overturn the conclusion).
- **Release**: generator, COVTYPE annotations, harness, configs, splits.

### 7.6 Ablations and mechanism validation

- D0 → D3 ladder (§5.3) on all battlefields; backbone-agnosticism (×3 backbones); nuisance quality (LightGBM vs MLP vs deliberately degraded — orthogonality should make D2 robust, Prop. 3's empirical check); treatment-basis misspecification (linear head vs nonlinear-truth generator); cross-fitting scheme (purged vs naive folds — leakage probe); γ̂/δ̂ dial sweeps; per-segment Θ heatmaps vs econometric estimates (interpretability deliverable).

## 8. Expected Risks, Limitations, Falsification

| Risk | Trigger | Mitigation / fallback |
|---|---|---|
| **H1 fails** (SOTA already intervention-valid) | G1 greenlight rule (i) unmet at γ̂ | preregistered pivot: negative-result audit ("when are forecasters accidentally causal?") — mechanistically interesting, modest-venue publishable; ≤ 2 weeks sunk |
| **Prior art exists** | G0 finds a 2024–26 paper covering audit or method | drop the covered contribution, re-scope (audit-only / theory-only / head-to-head extension); G0 runs before code for exactly this reason |
| **"CRN on retail" review** | novelty challenged at review time | head-to-head ports with documented adaptations; Props. 1–2 (identification for *direct forecasters* and the plan-ahead/adaptive split) have no analogue in that literature; frontier framing makes the difference quantitative |
| **Semi-synthetic realism contested** | "your generator, your win" | calibration to real-data statistics published; multiple generators (linear/nonlinear, M5-/Favorita-calibrated); real-data natural experiments carry the external-validity weight; everything released |
| **Weak identification on real data** | price variation sparse; policy too predictable (no overlap) | identification diagnostics reported first; claims scoped accordingly; M5 has cross-store price variation and SNAP's exogenous calendar; worst case the real-data leg is demoted to diagnostics + NEE-only |
| **No counterfactual ground truth on real data** | inherent | triangulation: semi-synthetic (exact), natural experiments (quasi-experimental), sign/monotonicity audits (weak but real); never claim more than the evidence class supports |
| **Effect sizes small in aggregate metrics** | scenario bias real but WMAPE-invisible | PRF and NEE are the decision-relevant readouts; the audit's *point* is that aggregate observational metrics hide the failure |
| **V2 (feedback) unfixable in direct forecasters** | markdown-heavy segments | estimand honesty: restrict scenario API to origin-decided paths; sequential variant scoped as extension; the limitation is itself a finding (Prop. 1) |
| **Scoop risk** | industrial demand-science groups move first | G0 sweep; arXiv the audit + diagnostics early (they are the unstaked claims); monitor arXiv monthly |
| **Theory dismissed as textbook** | "g-formula is 40 years old" | agreed, and stated; the contribution is the mapping to the direct multi-horizon contract, the V1/V2 decomposition with benchmark diagnostics, and the orthogonal path-treatment loss — claims scoped to that |

**Limitations (to be stated in the paper).** (1) Intervention validity is identified only up to the stated assumptions; hidden confounding is addressed by sensitivity analysis, not eliminated. (2) The linear-in-basis response class trades expressiveness for identification; nonlinear extensions are future work. (3) Real-data causal validation is quasi-experimental, not experimental. (4) Results are retail-domain; transfer to energy/pricing domains is asserted only as plausible. (5) We stop at valid scenarios; closed-loop pricing optimization raises strategic/feedback issues out of scope.

**Ethics note.** Demand/pricing research on public datasets; no deployment claims. Intervention-valid demand models could inform pricing that affects consumers; we discuss dual use briefly and release evaluation tools (audit), not pricing policies.

## 11. Topic-Selection Memo (why this topic; alternatives rejected)

Candidates surveyed against four criteria — novelty at top venues, underexploredness, feasibility with `input/` + vendored baselines, and hard disjointness from PRISM:

| Candidate | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Mixed-frequency / ragged-edge deep nowcasting (FRED-MD + daily markets) | re-application risk: irregular-sampling literature (ReIMTS ICLR'26, TimeCHEAT, Time-IMM — all in the index) covers asynchronous observation; the genuinely new part (publication lags/vintages) needs ALFRED data not in repo and lands between ML and econ reviewer cultures |
| Per-covariate lead-lag alignment for MISO | substantially covered by LIFT (ICLR'24, leading-indicator estimation); conceptually adjacent to the channel-dependence axis PRISM already gates on |
| Conformal / covariate-conditional coverage for MISO | crowded, fast-moving (ACI → SPCI → HopCPT → …); marginal-gain papers now routine; weak top-venue ceiling |
| Privileged-information distillation (train-rich, deploy-poor covariates) | clean but thin; plausible workshop-to-borderline-conference contribution |
| Decision-focused (newsvendor) end-to-end training | overlaps PRISM's decision-aware-loss ingredient; crowded in OR/DFL literature |
| Temporal-hierarchy coherence for a single target | IJF-flavored; moderate ML-venue novelty |

The interventional-validity topic won on all four criteria: the failure mode is unmeasured (novel), the bridging literature has never met the forecasting benchmark culture (underexplored), every required asset is already in the repo (feasible), and the question is orthogonal to architecture choice (disjoint from PRISM by construction).

## 12. Glossary

| Term | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| **MISO** | multiple-input single-output forecasting: many covariates in, one target out |
| **Controllable covariate `a`** | known-future input chosen by a decision-maker (price, promotion); the object of scenario queries |
| **Exogenous known covariate `c`** | known-future input not chosen in response to the target (calendar, SNAP schedule) |
| **Scenario query / intervention** | `E[y | V_t, do(a = a′)]` — forecast under a *set* covariate path, Pearl's do-operator |
| **Confounding (V1)** | the policy generating `a` shares causes with `y` that the model does not observe |
| **Within-horizon feedback (V2)** | components of `a` inside the horizon react to realized outcomes (markdowns) |
| **Plan-ahead policy** | action path fully decided at forecast origin given `V_t` (Prop. 1 condition A1) |
| **g-computation / g-formula** | Robins' identification formula for sequential interventions; the estimand a valid scenario forecaster implements |
| **Neyman orthogonality / DML** | loss construction first-order insensitive to nuisance-model error, via cross-fitting (Chernozhukov et al.) |
| **R-learner** | residual-on-residual orthogonal loss for heterogeneous effects (Nie & Wager); our multi-horizon loss's ancestor |
| **Elasticity surface Θ(H,c)** | per-horizon, history-conditional response coefficients — DoCast's interpretable output |
| **SER / PRF / NEE** | sign-error rate; policy-ranking fidelity (Kendall-τ over candidate plans); natural-experiment error vs DiD estimate |
| **DiD** | difference-in-differences quasi-experimental effect estimate (SNAP cross-state calendars) |
| **WRMSSE** | weighted root mean squared scaled error — M5 competition's official metric |
| **Purged cross-fitting** | temporally blocked nuisance folds with an embargo ≥ H, preventing leakage in orthogonalization |

## 13. Elevator Pitch

> "Every covariate-aware forecaster ships with a what-if API it cannot honor: ask TFT or TimeXer what happens to demand if you cut the price 20%, and you get the answer to a different question — what demand *was* like when the planner *chose* to cut prices, promotions riding on anticipated demand spikes and markdowns chasing realized sales. We give the first audit showing how wrong those scenario answers are on public benchmarks (often wrong in *sign*), a two-line identification theory saying exactly when a direct multi-horizon forecaster is secretly a valid causal engine, and DoCast: a structural head plus an orthogonalized loss that any backbone can adopt, recovering intervention-valid demand curves at almost no cost in ordinary accuracy. Forecasts are bought to make decisions; DoCast is forecasting that survives the decision being made."
